The DJI Avata 360's two fisheye lenses point up and down, so the stitch seam lies on the horizon, exactly where a garage's walls, pillars and markers are. The report therefore skips DJI's stitch, reconstructs the raw fisheyes as a calibrated rig, fixes metric scale with AprilTag markers and measured distances, and trains the splat on the same metric poses. Eight interactive widgets are placed next to the sections they explain.

Top risks

  • False loop closures between identical pillars fold the map while reprojection error stays low.
  • Unmeasured rolling-shutter readout, inter-lens baseline and factory-calibration accuracy.
  • Dim, flickering light and compression erase concrete texture.
  • Indoor flight safety: ATTI drift, failsafe without GNSS, link loss.
  • Licences: OpenMVS AGPL; PGSR, INRIA 3DGS, GOFish, MASt3R non-commercial.

What it teaches: the two lenses have different optical centres, so a stitcher that pretends they share one leaves a disparity that grows as 1/d. Project link: with b unpublished (UNVERIFIED, plausibly 2–4 cm), a pillar 1–2 m away is displaced by tens of pixels in stitched 8K equirect, which bundle adjustment cannot model. That is why the recommended pipeline treats the two fisheyes as a rig (§1.2.2, §4.1). Slide d₀ to see that tuning the stitch to one distance only moves the zero.

Pinhole projection playground

What it teaches: $\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{K}[\mathbf{R}\mid\mathbf{t}]\tilde{\mathbf{X}}$ with $u = f X_c/Z_c + c_x$; increasing f zooms in, yaw is the extrinsic rotation. Switch to the equidistant fisheye ($r = f\theta$) and push the point behind the camera: the fisheye still images it up to θ ≈ 100°, the pinhole cannot (Z ≤ 0). Project link: COLMAP's perspective and fisheye models reject rays with θ ≥ 90°, and this lens's horizon band lies at 90°–105°, hence the pinhole virtual-view rig (§2.1.2, §4.1.1). Scene: pillars every 4 m, floor 1.5 m below the camera.

Here each of the two rays gets independent noise σ, so the angular disparity noise is √2·σ and the Monte-Carlo value sits ≈ 1.4× above Z²σ_θ/B.

What it teaches: depth uncertainty grows with the square of distance and shrinks with baseline, σ_Z ≈ Z²σ_θ/B. Project link: the 3 m motion-baseline preset at Z = 5 m reproduces the report's ≈ 4 mm lower bound (§4.5.3); the ~3 cm inter-lens baseline gives errors hundreds of times larger, which is why b cannot provide scale (§4.3) and markers are needed.

What it teaches: an image as a sum of anisotropic Gaussians (mean, covariance, colour) optimised by gradient descent (Adam) on a photometric loss, with adaptive density control that clones Gaussians with large positional gradients and splits large ones, under a cap like the MCMC variant's. Simplified: 2D, additive blending without depth ordering. Project link: a good photometric fit says nothing about whether the Gaussians sit on true surfaces, which is why the splat is anchored to the metric mesh rather than used as the measurement (§2.4.5, §5.5).

Equirectangular ↔ cubemap mapper

cube face

Arrow keys move the probe when the map has focus. Dashed red line: on this drone the stitch seam lies on the horizon (latitude 0).

What it teaches: ERP maps longitude/latitude linearly, so a pixel at latitude θ covers a solid angle proportional to cos θ: the poles are hugely oversampled. A cubemap face is an ordinary perspective image; the mapping uses the report's convention $u = \tfrac{W}{2\pi}(\phi+\pi)$, $v = \tfrac{H}{\pi}(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\theta)$. Project link: on the Avata 360 the lens centres are the zenith and nadir (ERP's stretched rows) and the seam is the equator, the opposite of the usual "seam at the back" layout (§1.2.3, §2.5.1). The 1920 px face is simply 7680/4 for illustration.

Widget

Direction-dependent motion blur on the dual fisheye

Mean blur per lens ring (red = above the 2 px drop threshold)
Mean blur per cube face

What it teaches: a direction r moves across the image sphere at |ṙ| = |ω × r + (v − (v·r)r)/d|, and blur ≈ 18.3 px/deg × |ṙ| × t_exp (§3.2.1). Project link: with lenses up and down, a yaw turn smears the rim/horizon band (≈ 5.5 px at 30°/s and 1/100 s) while the lens centres stay sharp; forward motion near the floor smears the nadir instead. A single per-frame score cannot represent this, hence per-lens, per-sector selection with rings at 60° and 85° (§3.2.3, §6.1.6). Room model: floor at the set height, ceiling 1.5 m above the drone, side walls 3 m away, aisle ends 15 m away.
